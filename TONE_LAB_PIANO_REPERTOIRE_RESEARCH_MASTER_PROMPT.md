# TONELAB — GLOBAL PIANO REPERTOIRE RESEARCH

## MASTER RESEARCH INSTRUCTIONS

**Project:** ToneLab  
**Research type:** Global music repertoire discovery  
**Primary objective:** Build an extensive, diverse, internationally representative database of songs and musical compositions that could potentially be adapted for piano education.

---

## 1. ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

You are acting as an international music researcher and music cataloguing specialist.

Your task is to conduct extensive internet research and build a comprehensive collection of songs, musical compositions, themes and melodies from different musical cultures, geographical regions, historical periods and popular music genres.

The purpose of this research is to create a repertoire database for ToneLab, a music education project focused on teaching piano and other instruments through a combination of contemporary popular music, traditional music, film and game music, and classical repertoire.

**The primary objective is DISCOVERY, not pedagogical selection.**

Do not restrict your research to compositions that are already written for piano, easy to play, or commonly used in music education.

We deliberately want to discover a very broad range of music. The final pedagogical evaluation, selection, adaptation and arrangement will be performed separately by a professional music educator.

Do not exclude a composition because:
- It was originally written for another instrument.
- It requires substantial piano arrangement.
- It belongs to an unfamiliar or geographically distant musical tradition.
- It is not commercially successful or internationally famous.
- It is technically difficult.
- It is performed primarily by ensembles, orchestras or vocalists.
- It is relatively unknown outside its country or cultural region.

A composition does not need to have an existing piano version to be included.

---

## 2. RESEARCH PRINCIPLES

Follow these principles throughout the entire research process.

### 2.1 Global coverage

Research music from all continents and as many countries and cultural regions as reasonably possible.

Do not treat English-language music as the default or dominant reference point.

Search using:
- English.
- Local languages.
- Regional music terminology.
- Original song titles and transliterations.
- Local music publications and databases.
- Regional streaming and music discovery platforms where accessible.

### 2.2 Discovery over popularity

Include both internationally famous and relatively unknown music.

Give particular attention to:
- Regional artists.
- Independent musicians.
- Traditional and folk performers.
- Local popular music scenes.
- Regional musical movements.
- Music that is rarely represented in European music education.
- Older recordings and historical popular music.
- Contemporary local music scenes.

Do not automatically prioritize Western commercial chart success.

### 2.3 No premature pedagogical filtering

Do not decide whether a composition is suitable for beginners, intermediate or advanced piano students.

Do not discard compositions because of their perceived difficulty.

Do not assign artificial pedagogical value to compositions at this stage.

Collect reliable musical and historical information where available. Pedagogical assessment will be performed later.

### 2.4 Authenticity

Whenever possible, identify the original artist, composer, recording or traditional source.

Distinguish between:
- Original composition.
- Traditional or anonymous composition.
- Traditional melody with a known modern arrangement.
- Cover version.
- Adaptation.
- Film/game soundtrack recording.
- A song strongly associated with a particular artist but written by someone else.

Do not incorrectly attribute traditional music to a modern performer.

## 6. DATA COLLECTION REQUIREMENTS

Every discovered composition should be entered as a separate record.

Use CSV as the primary data format.

Recommended fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| id | Unique record identifier |
| title_original | Original title |
| title_english | English title or translation, if applicable |
| artist | Performing artist or group |
| composer | Composer, when identifiable |
| songwriter | Songwriter, when identifiable |
| country | Country of origin or strongest cultural association |
| region | Geographical region |
| language | Original language |
| genre | Main musical genre |
| subgenre | More specific musical style |
| musical_tradition | Traditional, popular, classical, soundtrack, etc. |
| decade | Original release or composition decade |
| year | Exact year, when verified |
| original_format | Solo, vocal, ensemble, orchestral, electronic, etc. |
| original_instruments | Known original instrumentation |
| piano_version_exists | Yes / No / Unknown |
| piano_arrangement_potential | Not assessed |
| source_url | Primary or most reliable source |
| audio_url | Link to an accessible recording, if available |
| additional_source_urls | Additional verification sources |
| source_language | Language of the research source |
| verification_status | Verified / Partially verified / Unverified |
| research_notes | Relevant factual information |

Additional fields may be introduced if genuinely useful, but maintain consistency across all files.

Do not invent missing information.

Use empty fields or explicitly mark unknown values.

Do not confuse:
- Artist country with song origin.
- Recording year with composition year.
- Release date with traditional historical origin.
- Original performer with composer.
- Genre with regional origin.

When geographical attribution is uncertain, explain the uncertainty in the notes.

## 7. SOURCES AND VERIFICATION

Use a variety of reliable sources.

Potential sources include:
- Official artist and composer websites.
- Record labels.
- Music encyclopedias.
- Discogs.
- MusicBrainz.
- AllMusic.
- Regional music publications.
- Local cultural institutions.
- National archives.
- Film and game credits.
- Soundtrack databases.
- Reputable music journalism.
- Official streaming platform pages.
- Regional music databases.

You may use YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music and other platforms to discover recordings and identify relevant material.

However, do not treat an automatically generated playlist or an unverified user upload as sufficient evidence for historical or authorship claims.

Where possible, verify important metadata using at least one independent source.

Every record must contain at least one useful source URL.

For uncertain information, use `Partially verified` or `Unverified`.

Never fabricate:
- Song titles.
- Artists.
- Composers.
- Countries of origin.
- Release dates.
- Genre classifications.
- Source URLs.

A plausible-sounding result is not a verified result.

## 8. RESEARCH WORKFLOW

Conduct research systematically, in multiple geographical and thematic batches.

Do not attempt to complete the entire global database in a single search query.

For each geographical region:

1. Identify important countries and local music scenes.
2. Research internationally known artists and compositions.
3. Discover regional and lesser-known performers.
4. Explore traditional and folk repertoires.
5. Explore contemporary popular music.
6. Search for genres and subgenres that are not widely represented internationally.
7. Identify representative songs and compositions.
8. Verify available metadata and sources.
9. Add records to the appropriate CSV file.
10. Check for duplicates and inconsistent classifications.
11. Record research gaps and areas requiring additional exploration.

Search in the original language whenever possible.

Do not stop researching a region simply because its internationally famous artists have been covered.

The goal is broad representation, not a list of the most popular songs on the internet.

## 10. RESEARCH DEPTH AND QUANTITY

There is no strict maximum number of compositions.

Do not artificially limit the research to 10, 20 or 50 examples per country or genre.

The target is an extensive database containing hundreds, and potentially thousands, of unique compositions.

However, quantity must never be achieved by sacrificing accuracy.

Prioritize:
- Geographic diversity.
- Genre diversity.
- Historical diversity.
- Cultural diversity.
- Diversity of artists and composers.
- Representation of lesser-known music.

Avoid repeatedly collecting similar songs from the same internationally famous artists while leaving entire countries and musical traditions unexplored.

When research becomes repetitive, move to a less explored genre, country or historical period.

---

## 11. IMPORTANT RESTRICTIONS

Do not:
- Filter compositions based on piano difficulty.
- Restrict research to existing piano arrangements.
- Generate fictional examples.
- Invent source links.
- Create arrangements or sheet music during this research phase.
- Download copyrighted sheet music or recordings without authorization.
- Reproduce copyrighted lyrics or full sheet music.
- Treat popularity as the only measure of musical relevance.
- Treat all traditional music from a region as stylistically identical.
- Replace original titles with English translations.
- Remove unusual or unfamiliar musical traditions simply because they are difficult to classify.

This project is a discovery and documentation process, not an arrangement-production process.

## 13. FINAL OBJECTIVE

The final result should be an extensive, well-organized, internationally diverse music repertoire database.

It should represent music that students in Serbia might rarely encounter through conventional music education.

The collection should include music they already know, music they may discover through the research, and music from cultures they might otherwise never encounter.

The database will subsequently be reviewed by a professional music educator who will determine:

- Which compositions are worth including in the teaching repertoire.
- Which compositions are appropriate for particular learning stages.
- Which compositions require piano arrangements.
- Which musical and technical competencies they can develop.
- Which compositions should be used for listening, analysis, improvisation or performance.

**Your responsibility is to discover, document and organize as much authentic and diverse music as possible. Do not make those pedagogical decisions on behalf of the educator.**

Begin by inspecting the existing research folder and its available instructions.

Then establish the directory structure, document the research methodology in `README.md`, and begin systematic research across all defined regions and thematic categories.

Work in manageable batches, save your results continuously, and maintain accurate progress reports.

Do not stop after producing an initial sample or a short list of recommendations. Continue systematic research until the available research budget or practical execution limits require a pause.

At that point, leave the database in a consistent state and clearly document where research should resume.
