## What this batch covers

Brazil: bossa nova, samba, MPB, Tropicália, choro, baião, forró (xote), frevo, sertanejo, axé, funk carioca. Argentina: tango, nuevo tango, milonga, zamba, chacarera, carnavalito, one candombe written in Salta. Chile: nueva canción, one cueca setting, one 1980s rock song. Colombia: vallenato, cumbia, salsa. Peru: vals criollo, the 1913 Andean pasacalle *El cóndor pasa*, Afro-Peruvian *Toro mata*, huayno. Venezuela: joropo (*Alma llanera*, *Moliendo café*) and the pasaje *Caballo viejo* (not labelled joropo). Uruguay: *La cumparsita*, candombe and milonga. Paraguay: guarania and polca paraguaya. Bolivia: saya (*Llorando se fue*). Ecuador: danzante, pasacalle, pasillo. Guyana: one string-band song. Suriname: two kaseko titles.

Local names used that are not in the bold taxonomy list: Guarania, Kaseko, Saya, Pasacalle, Danzante, Polca paraguaya, Carnavalito, Música llanera, Pasillo, String band. Chacarera, Axé, Funk carioca, Cueca, Huayno, Vallenato, Joropo and Cumbia were already listed.

## How verification was applied

Verified rows have the title, the main credit, the country and a year (or a clearly stated premiere) on at least two independent pages. Partially verified rows rest on one detailed page, on a single album booklet, or on sources that disagree about the year or the author. Year is empty where the consulted pages conflict or give no year (*La cumparsita*, *Vassourinhas*, *Pájaro campana*, *El plebeyo*, *El aguacate*, *Chacarera del rancho*, *Not a Blade of Grass*, *Srefidensi*, and the Folkways polcas).

*Moliendo café* has no composer in the row: José Manzo Perroni and Hugo Blanco are both credited in the sources. *Pájaro campana* is an arrangement of a disputed traditional melody, not a Pérez Cardozo composition. *Candombe para José* is by Roberto Ternán (Salta), not Rubén Rada. *Jejuí* is not dated, because a Paraguayan biography both links the guarania to 1925 and disputes the claim that Jejuí was the first one.

Songs written for a film and left out of this file: *Manhã de Carnaval* (*Orfeu Negro*) and *O que é que a baiana tem?* (*Banana da Terra*). Gardel’s film tangos, *El cóndor pasa* and *Alma llanera* stay here as tango, Andean and joropo repertoire, with the film or zarzuela origin in the notes. *Nuestro juramento* was not filed: it is a Puerto Rican bolero.
